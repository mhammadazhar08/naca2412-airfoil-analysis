Polars at four Reynolds numbers

Reynolds number barely changes lift in normal flight. It decides how late the airfoil stalls and how much drag it pays at high lift.

Pressure distribution

At α = −4° the stagnation point sits on the upper side of the nose and the suction peak (Cp = −1.67) forms on the lower surface, so lift is negative. Kinks in each curve mark where the boundary layer turns turbulent.

Validation against wind-tunnel data

The simulation is accurate in the linear range and optimistic near stall. Both errors trace back to XFoil's assumptions: a perfectly smooth surface and a simplified model of separation.

NACA 2412 vs NACA 0012

The NACA 0012 has the same 12% thickness and no camber, so every difference comes from camber alone. Camber lifts the whole curve by about 0.25 without changing its slope, and cuts drag where an aircraft actually cruises.

At Re = 1 million the gap grows: the 2412 has 28% less drag at Cl = 0.6 and a 35% higher best Cl/Cd. Camber trades a nose-down moment for efficiency, which is why wings use cambered sections and tails use symmetric ones.

Method

  1. Import geometry

    NACA 2412 coordinates loaded into XFLR5 v6.62 as a Selig-format .dat file.

  2. Refine the panels

    The 35-point file gave a pointed nose and a jagged pressure curve, so the section was rebuilt with 200 panels clustered at the leading and trailing edges.

  3. Run the polars

    Fixed-Reynolds analyses at 1, 3, 6 and 9 million, Mach 0.2, Ncrit 9, sweeping −5° to 20° in 0.5° steps.

  4. Extract and plot

    A Python script reads the polars straight out of the binary .xfl project; MATLAB and Python scripts plot them and compute lift slope, stall and best Cl/Cd.

  5. Verify and validate

    Pressure distributions integrated to check the reported lift, then results compared with NACA wind-tunnel data.

Problems solved along the way

Geometry

Too few panels distort the nose

35 points produced a sharp leading edge and kinks in Cp right where the suction peak forms. 200 panels fixed it; the lesson is to check geometry before trusting any number.

Sweep range

The first sweep stopped before stall

At 15° lift was still rising at every Re, so the "maximum" was not a maximum. Extending to 20° captured the real stall peak.

Convergence

Some angles would not solve

A few points failed where the transition point moves quickly. They are reported as gaps rather than forced or interpolated.
